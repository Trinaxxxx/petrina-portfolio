# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers and art directors at game studios and archvis / real-time visualisation studios. Split audience in roughly equal measure. They visit to assess whether Petrina can own the full pipeline — not just produce pretty renders, but solve the technical problems that slow a production down. The desired outcome is an interview, a trial brief, or a direct hire conversation.

Secondary audience: freelance clients who need environment or pipeline work scoped and executed independently.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio for Petrina Kinzel — Technical Environment Artist with 3 years of production experience. The site must communicate two things simultaneously: that she builds high-quality real-time environments, and that she understands the pipeline deeply enough to automate and improve it. The work shown spans CAD-to-real-time delivery, VR performance optimisation, live event deployment, and bespoke Blender Python tooling.

Success = a hiring manager from a game studio or archvis studio contacts Petrina for a role or project.

## Brand Personality

Sharp · Technical · Confident

Voice: direct, no hedging, no apology. Writes like someone who has already shipped the thing and is explaining how it works — not pitching. Tone is matter-of-fact with quiet confidence. No superlatives ("amazing", "cutting-edge"). Numbers and specifics over adjectives.

Emotional goal: the reader should feel they are reading a colleague's work log, not a student's application. Confidence without arrogance; depth without noise.

## Anti-references

- Generic ArtStation image-grid clone (no context, no story, just renders)
- Startup / SaaS landing page aesthetic (feature bullets, gradient CTAs, pricing-tier card grids)
- Student portfolio template (timid layout, everything centred, apologetic scale)
- AI-coded feel: cream background, Inter everywhere, eyebrow labels on every section, purple-to-blue gradients, identical cards in a grid, large rounded-square icon tiles above headings

Reference aesthetic: indie game dev / technical artist blog energy — dense, honest, shows the thinking not just the finished image. The kind of site where you learn something reading it, not just look at it.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the thinking, not just the result.** Every project should reveal the problem, the constraint, and the decision — not just the final render. A before/after with numbers is worth more than a beautiful image with no context.
2. **Technical depth is the brand.** The pipeline work, the tooling, the performance numbers — these are differentiators, not footnotes. They should be front and centre, not buried in a case study link.
3. **No ornamentation that doesn't earn its place.** Every visual element must serve legibility or hierarchy. If something is there to look impressive, remove it.
4. **Specificity over breadth.** Two well-documented projects beat six thumbnails. The site should feel complete for the work shown, not thin over many.
5. **Practice what you preach.** A technical artist who optimises pipelines should have a fast, clean, well-structured site. Performance and code quality are part of the portfolio.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA target throughout: ≥4.5:1 body text contrast, ≥3:1 large text and UI components. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — all scroll-triggered animations must have a fallback. Keyboard navigable. No content gated behind animation completion.
